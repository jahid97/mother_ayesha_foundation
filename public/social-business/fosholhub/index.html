FosholHub

A farmer-aggregator + market-linkage hub: aggregate smallholder produce, supply inputs + advisory + embedded finance, and connect farmers direct to buyers — so they keep more of the retail price and lose less to spoilage.
"The most competitive space in the portfolio — and the most useful: it's the aggregation layer our own ventures (FeedLoop, MycoGrow, FarmHalal) can ride on."
The problem

The "missing middle" eats the farmer's margin

Smallholders can produce but fail at aggregation, quality, finance and — fatally — a buyer. Middlemen capture the spread and post-harvest losses pile up.

27–45%of the retail price the farmer keeps today

The solution

Collection hubs that link farm to buyer

Local lead-farmers / collection agents run hubs that aggregate produce, sell quality inputs + advisory + embedded finance, and connect farmers direct to retailers, processors and institutional kitchens. The farmer produces; the hub solves grading, logistics, and demand.

The value chain

A chain — farm to market, fairly

Smallholders’ produce is aggregated, moved to market, and sold straight to buyers; the fair price flows back to farmers, cutting the middlemen — and brings more farmers in.

The model

A thin-margin volume business

One hub aggregates from ~200 smallholders moving ~BDT 2,000,000 GMV/month:

Honest framing: proven (iFarmer/WeGro) but crowded — a hub earns only a few % of GMV and is net-positive only above a throughput threshold; below it, logistics + cold-storage swamp the spread. Risk is differentiation + thin margins, not feasibility. Best as the aggregation layer for our own ventures. Upgrade: a women-run solar-drying service (S4S-style) recovers spoiling surplus as dried product and cuts the working-capital/spoilage risk → net base +25k rises to ≈ +57k/mo. Closes on: both-sided commitment on one crop/corridor.

Research & evidence

Bangladesh smallholders lose to middlemen and post-harvest waste; aggregation lifts income

The situation in Bangladesh

In Bangladesh, marginal and smallholder farmers make up roughly 83% of the farming community and work about half the cultivable land, yet most hold under 1.5 acres, leaving the sector deeply fragmented 6. Agriculture employs about 35–37% of the labor force but contributes only 11–12% of GDP, a gap that reflects how little value reaches the farm gate 7. Layers of middlemen capture most of that value: in Dhaka District farmers receive under 50% of the consumer price for key vegetables (around 33% for tomato, 39% for cauliflower), while consumers pay two to three times the farm-gate price 13. On top of this, post-harvest losses are severe, with vegetables losing about 25% and roughly one-third of all horticultural produce wasted for lack of storage, cold chain and transport 2.

Our pitch

FosholHub buys produce directly from smallholders, aggregates it, and sells to buyers, collapsing the intermediary chain that today drains farmer margins and inflates consumer prices 13. Aggregation through producer-organization models has measurable income effects: survey evidence shows membership raising marketed surplus 25%, production value 18%, and total income 15% 4. Better market linkage also lifts farm-gate prices directly, with internet and price-information access shown to win farmers up to 5% higher prices 8, while shorter chains and faster offtake cut the post-harvest spoilage that erases a quarter of vegetable value 2. Digital agri precedents in the region prove the model scales cheaply, with platforms reaching millions of farmers at well under a dollar each and benefit-cost ratios of 9–15 9, and Bangladeshi players like iFarmer and AgroMukam already validate demand for input-and-buyer connectivity 5. FosholHub's wedge is owning the aggregation and offtake layer rather than only publishing prices, turning information advantage into guaranteed purchase and a fairer split for the farmer.
